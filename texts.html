📚 Hindu Sacred Texts

Explore the timeless wisdom of India's ancient scriptures — guiding humanity toward truth and self-realization.

📖 Bhagavad Gita
Author: Sage Vyasa (as part of the Mahabharata)
Written in: Sanskrit
Approx. Period: 2nd century BCE

The *Bhagavad Gita* (“Song of God”) is a conversation between Lord Krishna and the warrior Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. It reveals deep spiritual truths about duty (dharma), righteousness, and devotion. The Gita teaches that one must act selflessly without attachment to the results of actions — the essence of *Karma Yoga*. It also describes *Bhakti Yoga* (path of devotion) and *Jnana Yoga* (path of knowledge), offering the ultimate guide to living a balanced spiritual life.
🕉️ Ramayana
Author: Sage Valmiki
Written in: Sanskrit
Approx. Period: Around 500 BCE

The *Ramayana* narrates the divine story of Lord Rama, his wife Sita, and his loyal companion Hanuman. It portrays the triumph of virtue over vice, symbolized by Rama’s victory over Ravana, the ten-headed demon king. Beyond being an epic, it’s a moral compass that emphasizes righteousness (*Dharma*), sacrifice, and love. The Ramayana has inspired countless adaptations in various languages, including the *Ramcharitmanas* by Tulsidas.
📜 The Vedas
Authors: Ancient Rishis (sages)
Written in: Vedic Sanskrit
Approx. Period: 1500–500 BCE

The *Vedas* are the foundation of Hindu philosophy and rituals. There are four main Vedas — *Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda,* and *Atharvaveda.* They contain hymns, chants, rituals, and philosophical ideas revealed to the sages through divine inspiration (*Shruti*). The Vedas emphasize cosmic order (*Rita*), sacrifice (*Yajna*), and knowledge (*Vidya*) as the path to divine realization. Each Veda is further divided into *Samhitas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas,* and *Upanishads*.
🔱 The Upanishads
Authors: Various sages and philosophers
Written in: Sanskrit
Approx. Period: 800–200 BCE

The *Upanishads* form the concluding portion of the Vedas, exploring the nature of ultimate reality (*Brahman*) and the individual soul (*Atman*). They teach that the essence of every being is divine and that realizing this unity leads to *Moksha* (liberation). Key concepts include “Tat Tvam Asi” (Thou art that) and “Aham Brahmasmi” (I am Brahman). Some of the principal Upanishads include *Isha, Kena, Chandogya,* and *Brihadaranyaka*.
